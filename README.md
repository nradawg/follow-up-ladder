**Use this when** someone went quiet on you and the next thing you send should
not be the words "just checking in."

## What I do

I write your next three touches. Each one has to carry something that was not
in the last one, and the third one closes the file. Then I stop, because a
fourth polite nudge is not persistence, it is a signal that you will keep
sending these whether or not they answer.

## The test every touch has to pass

Read the message as if they had never replied to you at all. Would it still be
worth opening? If the only content is that time has passed, it fails, and
rewriting it warmer does not fix it. Time passing is your news, not theirs.

## What counts as something new

| real | not real |
|---|---|
| the answer to the question they actually asked | a restated benefit |
| a thing that changed on your side, truthfully | a manufactured deadline |
| a smaller ask than last time | the same ask, friendlier |
| a specific person they should talk to instead | "wanted to bump this" |
| permission to close the file | "circling back on the below" |

Never invent urgency. A price increase that is not happening, a slot that is
not filling, a deadline you made up: that is a lie with a date attached, and it
is the one they can check.

## The three rungs

1. **Answer the open thread.** Whatever the last real question was, answer it
   in full with nothing attached. No ask. This is the only touch that is
   allowed to want nothing.
2. **Shrink the ask.** They did not say no to you, they said no to the size of
   the next step. Replace the meeting with a question they can answer from
   their phone, or with a yes or no.
3. **Close the file, and mean it.** Say you are stopping, say what would make
   you pick it back up, and then actually stop. This gets answered more than
   the two above it, and only because it is true.

Spacing: leave enough days that each message is a separate decision rather than
a stack in one thread. Tie it to whatever is moving on their side, a board
meeting, a quarter ending, the seat they are hiring. If nothing is moving on
their side, say that to yourself plainly, because then the honest ladder is
short.

## Limits

I cannot tell you why they went quiet, and almost always nobody can. The
ladder is built on the most likely reason, which means it is built on a guess,
and a wrong guess just means touch one lands flat rather than badly. I also
have no figures on reply rates by touch or by timing, and I will not supply
any. If you want yours, count your own sends.

The trap: three friendly nudges with no new information. The naive version
writes "following up," "bumping this," and "last try," each one polite and each
one teaching them that ignoring you has no cost. Worse, the naive version
reaches for fake urgency to break the silence, which works once and burns the
relationship when they find out the deadline was decoration.

## How this one knows what it knows

Every answer it gives itself carries a label: `CHECKED` (it verified it,
and says how), `FROM YOU` (you said it, and it quotes where), or
`GUESSED` (it could not verify). Then it counts the guesses, so you
correct what is wrong instead of trusting confident output built on
silent assumptions.

> Never ask for something you can check.
> Never assume something you cannot check.
